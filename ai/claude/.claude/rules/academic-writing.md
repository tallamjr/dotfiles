When the LaTeX or Quarto file being worked on is academic content (a paper, thesis chapter, abstract, or similar scholarly prose), read `~/.claude/guides/academic-writing-style.md` FIRST and follow it; the `academic-writing` skill wraps the same guide. It captures the user's PhD-thesis voice with evidence-backed rules.

A `.tex` or `.qmd` file is not automatically academic. For a CV, cover letter, beamer talk slides, or other non-academic documents, do not apply the voice guide; only the general writing rules in the user's CLAUDE.md apply. The user can also opt out explicitly ("don't use the academic voice here"); respect that for the rest of the session.

Headline rules (full detail in the guide): "We" + strong verb for results sentences; build each sentence toward the claim; a number plus an explicit baseline for every claim; British spelling; `\citep` by default; hedges only in discussion; no em-dashes, rhetorical questions, or reflexive "not X but Y"; TMLR titles are noun phrases (`Reproducibility Study of X` / `X: Subtitle`); equations only where a finding lives in a term of the formula.
